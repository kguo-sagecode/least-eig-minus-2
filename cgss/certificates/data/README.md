# Certificate format

Every computation file is JSON with `schema: "linegraphs-sage-v1"`, a
`computations` list and `sage_version`. Entries are JSON integers or reduced
rationals `{"num": a, "den": b}` with `b > 1`; there are no floating-point
entries. Strings describe conventions and formulas, not executable proof code.
All indices are zero-based. Matrices are arrays of rows. Polynomial coefficient
lists are in increasing degree, including internal zeros.

These are certificate **inputs** for Lean. Sage checking them does not put Sage
inside the trust base. No Lean importer or soundness theorem is supplied here;
several of the Lean computations in `LineGraphs/Computations/` take their data from
these files (transcribed, or generated by the scripts in `LineGraphs/scripts/`) and
check it in the kernel. [verify_certificates.sage](../verify_certificates.sage) reloads
the serialized values and checks the spectral, graph, design, LDL, root-map and
cross-file witnesses, and writes `manifest.json` in this directory: the SHA-256
hashes of the certificate files and of the source files of the run, and the
SageMath version. The manifest is not kept in the repository; each run writes it
again.

## Root indices and normalization

The explicit `labels` in [c02_models.json](c02_models.json) encode the structured
types in `LineGraphs/LineSystem/Models.lean`. They do not depend on the internal
enumeration order of Lean's `Fintype` instances. A consumer must reconstruct the
index from its label, or use the supplied list order throughout its checker.

* `AnIndex`: `[i,j]`, ordered distinct coordinates, nested increasing `i,j`.
* `DnIndex`: `[i,j,b,c]`, `i<j`, then lexicographic Boolean bits `b,c`.
  Bit 0 is `false` and has sign +1; bit 1 is `true` and has sign -1.
* `E8_D`: 112 labels `["D", [i,j,b,c]]`, followed by 128 labels
  `["half", [b0,...,b6]]`. The D vectors are doubled; the eighth half-root
  sign is the product of the first seven signs. The squared norm is 8, `q=4`.
* `E8_A`: 72 `["A",[i,j]]` labels, followed by lexicographically ordered
  `["triple",[i,j,k],b]`. Coordinates are tripled, the unsigned triple is -2
  on its support and +1 off it. Squared norm 18, `q=9`.
* `E8_S`: 16 `["axis",i,b]` labels, followed by
  `["block",[i,j,k,l],[b0,b1,b2,b3]]`. Blocks are exactly `steiner348`, in
  lexicographic order; signs follow the listed points. Squared norm 4, `q=2`.
* `E7` and `E6`: stable sublists of `E8_D`, retaining the same structured labels.
  E7 imposes `v[6]=v[7]`; E6 also imposes `v[5]=v[6]`.

In each model the normalized real root is `v/sqrt(q)`, of squared norm 2.
`line_representative_indices` select the root whose first nonzero coordinate is
positive. Counts of roots and lines are deliberately distinguished.

[c03_maps.json](c03_maps.json) uses row-vector maps:
`source[i] * matrix = target[permutation[i]]`. Its norm factor is
`q_target/q_source`: 4/9 for A→D and 2 for Steiner→D. Thus it checks equality of
normalized Gram matrices. The normalized A→D isometry is `(3/2)*matrix`, and the
normalized Steiner→D isometry is `matrix/sqrt(2)`. The latter is not a rational
matrix; the certificate itself is entirely rational in the integer encodings.

[c04.json](c04.json) refers to the root lists in [c06.json](c06.json) under the
same system names. Each `star_sum_indices` entry `[i,j,k]` means
`dot(v_i,v_j)=-q` and `v_i+v_j=v_k`. Gamma and Delta lists are root indices;
Gamma adjacency means **orthogonality**, following Section 2. A spanning-tree
edge refers to positions in the line-representative list. C6 uses
`frame = sum(v*v^T)/q = frame_factor * projector`; the projector is the identity
on the span, embedded in the specified ambient coordinates.

C20 lists every neighbourhood as root indices. Its apex pairs satisfy
`v_i+v_j=c`. C21 gives simple-root indices, their Gram matrix and inverse, and
the integral coordinates of every root in that basis. C13 additionally gives a
rational map from the printed Steiner vectors to the fixed Lean E7 sublist.

## Matrix and graph witnesses

A spectral packet has a symmetric integer `matrix`, distinct integer
`eigenvalues`, positive `multiplicities`, an `annihilator_coefficients` list, and
`traces[j] = tr(A^j)` for `j=0,...,number_of_distinct_eigenvalues-1`. The zeroth
trace is the order. A Lean checker needs polynomial evaluation and these traces,
then the spectral/Vandermonde soundness lemma. The larger
`characteristic_polynomial_coefficients` field is a Sage cross-check and is not
intended for kernel determinant expansion. In C13, `paper_Seidel` is `2A-J+I`.

A graph packet supplies `order`, sorted `edges`, and a redundant integer
`adjacency` matrix on `0,...,order-1`. For an isomorphism `p`,
`A_source[i,j] = A_target[p[i],p[j]]`. A switching witness first switches the
source with respect to `subset`, then applies `permutation`. The C13 `signs`
are -1 on that subset and +1 elsewhere. Named Sage targets are included as
explicit matrices, so checking a map never requires rerunning an isomorphism
search or reproducing Sage's vertex labels.

C22's `four_cliques_through_vertex` counts unordered 4-subsets containing each
vertex. Its sorted histogram distinguishes T8 and all three Chang graphs,
including II and III whose **global** four/five-clique totals agree. A claw is
stored as `[centre,leaf1,leaf2,leaf3]`, with sorted leaves.

An LDL packet has `matrix`, unit lower triangular `lower`, and positive
`diagonal`, satisfying `matrix = lower * diag(diagonal) * lower^T`. For Remark
3.11, this matrix is `diag(symmetrizer)*cartan`, in the paper's specified Mathlib
order. C19's 7x7 inverse witnesses instead satisfy
`matrix * inverse_numerator = inverse_denominator * I` (denominator 14).

C17 uses Sage affine Dynkin order `[0,1,...,rank]`; the affine node 0 is the
marked vertex. The positive vector satisfies `A*x=2*x`. The remaining-root Gram
matrix has positive LDL pivots. The list of all deletion determinants also
checks the stronger statement that any one vertex can be removed.

## Algebra, searches and designs

C1, C8, C10 and C11 encode multivariate polynomials as lists
`[[exponent_vector, coefficient], ...]`; the associated `variables` or
`formula_variables` list fixes the variable order. C11 rational functions have
separate numerator and denominator polynomials. Its formulas assume the
denominators nonzero, with `s>1,t>=1` in the design application. Its H instances
include both an integer matrix polynomial identity's parameters and a spectral
packet. C12 records its matrix assumptions and actual borders.

C8 stores every finite `(n,d)` case satisfying `1<=n<=28`, `0<=d<n`,
`n<2d+4`, split by the quadratic inequality. C14 has the full t=2 survivor list
and a bounded supplemental table for t=2–20; its general lower bound is symbolic.
C18 gives bounds, predicates, and all successive survivor lists. The two
complement predicates are essential. Lean should recompute the bounded search
and compare to `lemma_P`; a survivor list alone is not a proof of exhaustiveness.
C24 rows are `[m,left,right,gap]` for every integer m from 21 through 27.

Design `parameters` have order `[v,k,b,r,lambda]`. Blocks are lists of point
indices; block **positions** are the labels, so the format permits repeated
blocks. A resolution `classes` partitions the block indices, and each class's
blocks partition the points. Flag labels are `[point_index,block_index]`.

C23's projective coordinates use integer encodings of GF(4)/GF(8), with the
defining field polynomial provided in increasing degree. Projective points and
line coefficients are normalized to first nonzero coordinate 1. A design point
labels an external projective line; a design block labels a point off the
hyperoval. The biplane's block t is the translate by t modulo 37 of the recorded
fourth-power difference set. The residual deletes block 0 and its nine points.
